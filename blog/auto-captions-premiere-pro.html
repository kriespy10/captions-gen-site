Auto Captions in Premiere Pro, the Fast Way

Updated 2026-08-26 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

You don't have to type captions in Premiere Pro, and you haven't had to for years. Automatic transcription ships with the app, runs offline, and is free. What trips people up is the difference between the transcript and captions, and what the automatic route can and can't do to the look. Let's do the whole thing in four steps.

The 4-step auto caption workflow

  1. Transcribe: Window > Text → Transcript tab → Transcribe sequence. Language auto-detects; audio never leaves your machine.
  2. Generate captions: click Create captions. Format: Subtitle. Characters per line: 16–20 for the short-form look, 32–42 for documentary-style lines.
  3. Proofread: skim the caption track and fix names, brands and numbers — the usual speech-to-text casualties. Double-click a caption to edit text without touching timing.
  4. Style and export: one Track Style in Essential Graphics, then burn in from the export dialog's Captions tab.

Making auto captions more accurate

Three things move accuracy more than anything else: transcribe from the cleanest dialogue track (not the full mix with music), pick the language manually when you know it, and feed it decent audio — a noisy phone recording transcribes like a noisy phone recording. Expect to fix a handful of words per minute of speech, mostly proper nouns.

Where the built-in auto captions stop

Premiere's automatic captions know when a sentence is spoken, but the native caption track can't do anything with word-level timing — no highlighting the current word, no per-word animation, one static style per track. That's not a settings problem; the caption system just doesn't support it.

If that's the look you need, the options are converting captions to graphics and keyframing by hand (fine for one clip, brutal for thirty), or a plugin that transcribes with word timestamps and builds the animation for you.

For static subtitles, Premiere's auto captions are all you need and they're four clicks away. For animated captions, automatic word timing is the missing piece — and that's a plugin job, not a hidden setting.
