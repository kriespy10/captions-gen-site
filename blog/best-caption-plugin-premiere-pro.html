Which one to pick

  • You want CapCut- or Hormozi-style captions and hate subscriptions: Captions Gen or Caption Plug. Both transcribe locally, so footage stays on your machine.
  • You edit on a Mac: Caption Plug, the built-in captions, or one of the subscription plugins. Captions Gen is Windows-only for now.
  • You already pay for AutoCut, FireCut or Premiere Assistant: try their captions before buying anything else.
  • You only need plain subtitles or an .srt: Premiere's own captions. See how to add captions in Premiere Pro.
  • You want to test before paying: Captions Gen's free version (15 minutes of transcription, no watermark) and Premiere Assistant's free plan let you try on a real edit.

What actually matters

Local or cloud transcription

Cloud transcription uploads your client's footage and is usually metered by hours per month. Local transcription (Whisper on your machine) is private and has no meter.

Native graphics or rendered files

The good plugins place Motion Graphics clips you can open in Properties to fix a word or nudge timing. Rendered video overlays are dead the moment the edit changes.

Animation styles

At minimum: a highlighted spoken word (color swap and pill), pop or bounce on the word, and in/out animations. Presets that bundle font, color and animation save the most time.

Restyle speed

You will change your mind about the look. A plugin that restyles the whole sequence without transcribing again turns that into seconds.

Total cost

At $15 to $35 a month, a subscription costs more than a one-time plugin within one to two months. That's fine if you use the other features (silence cutting, multicam); it's poor value for captions alone.
