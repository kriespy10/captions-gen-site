Best fonts for captions and subtitles

Published 26 August 2026 · Updated 9 November 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Premiere Pro guide. Using After Effects? Install its panel and create your first captions.

The best caption font is one you can read on the final video without fighting the footage. Start with a clear family, check its language characters, then compare weight, size, line breaks and background treatment in a short export. A font name alone does not determine readability.

Start with a small, practical shortlist

Inter or Arial can be useful neutral starting points. Montserrat or Poppins can suit a heavier social-video treatment. Anton or Bebas Neue can provide condensed emphasis for short text. These are design suggestions to test, not a ranking backed by a reading-speed study.

Compare the exact available weights and the actual script you need. A family that works for short English words may have different coverage or line length in another language. Check the font source and license before sharing it with a client or bundling it in a project handoff.

Run a useful font test

  1. Choose a short real passage containing a long line, numbers, a name and the characters used by the language.
  2. Keep the footage, wording, position and timing the same while comparing two candidate fonts.
  3. Check the lightest and busiest shots, not only a still frame chosen for the text.
  4. Export at the intended resolution and watch at the expected size. For phone delivery, inspect it on a phone.
  5. Choose the result that remains clear, then check the rest of the captions for unusual wrapping.

This isolates the font decision better than comparing one family on clean footage with another on a bright, moving background. It also catches substitutions that the editing view may make easy to miss.

Choose size by visible height and line length

On a 1080 x 1920 edit, 55 to 75 pixels can be a starting experiment for a short social caption. It is not a universal recommendation or platform rule. Two fonts at the same numerical size can have different visible heights and widths.

Try the longest caption and the narrowest part of your framing. If it becomes three cramped lines, shorten the grouping or use a different line break before making every caption tiny. Keep enough clearance for the destination's controls and surrounding visual information.

Use weight and contrast deliberately

Very thin strokes or dense decorative forms can be difficult at small sizes. A heavier weight may help, but excessive outlines can close up letter shapes. Check the interior spaces in letters as well as the overall boldness.

Try a dark stroke, a shadow or a background when the image changes behind the text. Keep only what improves separation. A design that works on a dark shot may disappear on a white shirt or a bright sky, so test those transitions.

Check case, punctuation and language

All caps can be a stylistic choice for brief emphasis; sentence case can be useful for longer passages. Compare them with the actual text rather than asserting that one case always reads faster.

Check accents, apostrophes, currency symbols and any non-Latin script in the exported file. An apparently installed font can still fall back for missing characters. A subtitle file shown by a player may use the player's own font rather than the font selected in Premiere.

Apply the result in Premiere

For native captions, select a caption, open Properties and set the font and appearance. Save a Track Style to reuse the look. For a missing family or project-wide substitution, follow the font troubleshooting guide.

For animated graphics, review the active-word color and size as well as the base font. A word that grows can change the apparent spacing or cover nearby text. Captions Gen has saved design presets and Apply style for its generated batch, but its .cgpreset files do not include font files.

Keep delivery expectations clear

Burned-in captions and animated graphics retain their appearance as part of the video image. A basic SRT sidecar delivers text and timing, not the complete font design. Provide the right output and a short review sample when a client's requested appearance matters.
