How to burn in captions in Premiere Pro

Published 26 August 2026 · Updated 9 November 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Premiere Pro guide. Using After Effects? Install its panel and create your first captions.

For native captions, open Export, expand Captions and choose Burn Captions Into Video. Check the intended caption tracks and export range, then render a short sample. The words become part of the video picture, so a player does not need to load a separate subtitle file to display that text.

Graphics are different: Type-tool titles, upgraded captions and Captions Gen output render through video tracks. The native caption burn-in setting does not control those graphic clips.

Decide whether burn-in fits the delivery

Use burn-in when the delivered picture should retain a fixed caption design or the destination cannot take the required caption file. Use a native sidecar or supported embedded stream when the viewer or recipient needs separately selectable captions.

A client can request both a captioned video and a clean video with subtitle files. Label those versions clearly. Once words are burned into a delivered image, turning off a player's CC button will not remove them.

Burn a native caption track into the picture

  1. Open the intended final sequence and save the project.
  2. Enable the caption track or tracks meant to appear in this picture version. Disable unintended alternative-language tracks so they do not appear together.
  3. Check the caption text and timing in the Program Monitor, including a long line and a bright background shot.
  4. Open Export and expand Captions. Choose Burn Captions Into Video and inspect the available Caption Tracks.
  5. Set the correct export range, format and destination filename. Follow the recipient's picture and audio requirements.
  6. Export a short passage and open that actual new file. Verify that the text is visible without loading an SRT.

Adobe documents burn-in for active caption tracks. If no relevant track appears, check whether your text is native captions or graphics, and confirm you opened the correct sequence.

If the captions are graphics

Keep the graphic clips inside the export range with their video-track output enabled. Check text-layer visibility, opacity, animation boundaries and any opaque layer above the graphic. Export through the ordinary video rendering workflow.

Captions Gen's Premiere Motion Graphics and After Effects caption layers use this picture-based output. They do not create a selectable caption stream or SRT sidecar. If required, retain the native caption workflow alongside the animation.

Check quality at delivery size

Caption pixels are compressed with the rest of the video. Inspect fine strokes, outlines and small letters in the actual export, especially after scaling or a low-quality encode. No universal bitrate or font size guarantees readable text for every codec and frame size.

Choose an appropriate export preset for the destination, then test the longest caption over changing footage. For a vertical social version, check a phone and the app's interface. See vertical caption placement and font readability for those design checks.

Keep a clean master when later changes are likely

If a colourist, translator or client will create another version, keep a sequence or master without burned-in text. A burned-in typo cannot be removed by deleting an SRT. Return to the source project, correct the caption and export a new delivery.

When also supplying subtitle files, export each required language track and keep it matched to the video version. The SRT guide explains the separate file handoff. Burn-in alone does not replace specified broadcast closed-caption data.

If text is missing or duplicated

If it is absent in the preview, check track visibility and caption time ranges before export. If it appears in the preview but not the file, inspect the export source, chosen caption option and intended track. Use a new filename for the test so an older file is not mistaken for the corrected result.

If words appear twice, check for both graphics and native burn-in, multiple enabled language tracks or a receiving app adding another subtitle layer. Inspect which text is already part of the video before changing the caption file.

The missing-export guide separates native burn-in, sidecar, embedded and graphic cases. Check the final delivered file after the full export, not only the short test.
