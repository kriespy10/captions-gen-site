Closed captions in Premiere Pro

Published 26 August 2026 · Updated 9 November 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Premiere Pro guide. Using After Effects? Install its panel and create your first captions.

For closed-caption delivery in Premiere Pro, choose the recipient's required caption format and export method. CEA-608, CEA-708, an SRT sidecar and visible burned-in text are not interchangeable specifications. Ask for the required format, channel or service, and output container before creating the delivery track.

Get the delivery details first

Record the exact picture version, frame rate, start timecode, caption language and required file type. Confirm whether the recipient wants an embedded caption stream, a separate caption file, a visible-text review copy or several of these.

CEA-608 is an older caption standard and CEA-708 provides digital caption services. That distinction does not tell you which one a particular broadcaster accepts. Follow the supplied specification rather than choosing whichever export option appears first.

Create and review the caption track

  1. Open the final matching sequence and save a working copy.
  2. Create a reviewed transcript or import the supplied compatible caption file. Correct names and other transcription errors before making the delivery version.
  3. In Create captions, choose the specified Format and relevant stream options. Check the settings against the brief before creating the track.
  4. Review text, timing, speaker identification and meaningful sound descriptions required by the programme. Automatic recognition of speech alone does not complete that editorial work.
  5. Check that the intended caption track is active. Keep alternate language or review tracks identifiable.

Format restrictions affect available styling and characters. Test the actual delivery format rather than designing a freely styled Subtitle track and assuming every property will survive conversion. The subtitle and caption-format guide explains that distinction.

Choose a supported sidecar

Adobe documents different supported tracks for different export extensions. SCC supports CEA-608 tracks. MCC supports CEA-608 and CEA-708. XML and STL have their own supported track combinations. Check the exact required extension and track, not just the existence of a sidecar file.

If the requested option is missing, inspect both the caption-track format and export settings. Renaming an SRT file to SCC or MCC does not convert its contents. Return to the correct format or obtain the required file through an appropriate caption workflow.

Choose supported embedded output

Embedded captions require a compatible caption format and container. Adobe lists MOV and specified MXF containers among its supported embedding/decoding formats. This does not mean every codec and preset in those containers meets every delivery specification.

Open Export and inspect the available caption tracks and embedded options. Keep the specified channel/service selected and check other required picture and audio settings separately. Use a short sample to confirm the receiving workflow can read the stream.

Verify the exported deliverable

Watch the actual exported video in a player that supports the delivered caption format, with captions enabled. Load sidecars explicitly when needed. Check the beginning, several speaker changes, pauses and the ending against the final edit.

For a formal handoff, obtain the recipient's confirmation or required quality-control result. Seeing words in Premiere's Program Monitor only confirms the preview. It does not establish that the exported stream is present or accepted.

Keep a visible-text review copy separate

Burn-in makes captions part of the picture and can help a reviewer see the wording. It cannot replace a required selectable stream or sidecar. Label a review copy clearly so it is not mistaken for the final embedded-caption master.

If captions are missing from a file, use the export troubleshooting guide. If timing is wrong in both preview and export, correct the caption track before generating another delivery.

Animated graphics are another output

Captions Gen 1.1.9 creates animated Premiere graphics or After Effects layers. It does not generate a native broadcast caption stream or SRT sidecar. Keep the required native caption workflow alongside the animated version, and copy later wording corrections into each deliverable.
