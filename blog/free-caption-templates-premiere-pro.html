Free caption templates for Premiere Pro

Published 22 October 2026 · Updated 9 November 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Premiere Pro guide. Using After Effects? Install its panel and create your first captions.

A free caption template can mean three different things in Premiere Pro: a MOGRT graphic, a native caption Track Style, or a speech-driven plugin preset. Before downloading one, decide whether you need reusable design, selectable subtitle text, or animated word-by-word graphics.

This guide separates graphic templates, native caption styles and speech-driven plugin presets. It also covers the 18 currently free animated presets in Captions Gen for Premiere Pro on Windows.

In short

  • MOGRT text templates: reusable graphics; text and timing need another workflow.

  • Captions Gen presets: combine text grouping, appearance and animation with locally generated word timings.

The three kinds of caption templates

1. MOGRT text templates

A MOGRT (Motion Graphics Template) provides a reusable graphic, often with editable text and design controls. Add it to the timeline and fill in the controls its author exposed.

It can work well for a title or lower third. A MOGRT alone doesn't transcribe the dialogue or decide where every caption belongs, so plan how you'll supply and time the words.

2. Premiere Track Styles

Premiere Pro can save a caption look as a Track Style. This feature is included with the paid Adobe application and applies the appearance across a caption track.

The catch: Track Styles are static. There's no highlight on the spoken word and no animation.

3. Caption presets in a plugin

Captions Gen transcribes your audio locally on the CPU, then uses its preset settings to group the words and build timed Motion Graphics clips. Some presets use spoken-word highlights; calmer ones use simpler text or fades.

Review the words and timing before delivery. The generated graphics render into the picture, so use a separate native caption track when you need SRT or closed captions.

How to use them

  1. Get the free Windows setup directly, with no email address or payment card required. Save your work, close the Adobe apps, then choose Premiere Pro in setup. You need Premiere Pro 25.6 or newer on supported 64-bit Windows 10 or 11, Creative Cloud Desktop and Adobe installation permissions.
  2. Reopen Premiere Pro and go to Window > UXP Plugins > Captions Gen. Let any missing speech models download before generating; allow at least 3 GB for the built-in models. Later transcription uses the cached models on your CPU.
  3. Select the clip with the voice in your timeline.

Use the full Setup for a first installation. Check the current product requirements and 1.1.9 release notes if you're updating an existing installation.

  1. Tap a preset on the Presets tab. The big preview plays it.
  2. Press Generate Captions and check the words before placement if transcript review is enabled.

Your captions appear as editable graphics above your clip. Review names, line breaks and word highlights before exporting.

Changed your mind about the look? Pick another preset and use Apply style for the placed captions. Appearance changes can reuse the existing words; changing grouping can require transcription again. After correcting text, use Update animation when offered and check the timing.

Make your own template

Most editors build one style for their channel and use it on every video. Here's how:

  1. Pick the preset that's closest to what you want.
  2. Open Customize and change the font, colors, size, outline, shadow or animation.
  3. Open Save preset beside the preview, choose Save as preset or Save as new preset, and give it a name.
  4. Open the saved tile's ⋯ menu and choose Make main preset. The panel opens on that preset next time.

Share it with your team

Open a preset's ⋯ menu to export a .cgpreset file. On another installation, use Import. The current panels use the same preset settings, while Premiere creates graphics and After Effects creates native layers. Check a short sample in each host rather than expecting identical rendering.

Send your team the exact font family and weight as well as the preset. Font metadata identifies the desired font; it does not install or license the font. Test a long word and a non-Latin character if the project uses them.

The 1.1.9 combined Windows installer offers Premiere Pro, After Effects or Both, sharing the engine and Models folder. For composition work, follow the After Effects installation guide, then editing captions in After Effects. The steps above cover Premiere Pro.

Check a template before using it

  • Deliverable: decide whether the client needs visible graphics, SRT text or both. A rendered caption design does not create a selectable caption track.
  • Format: import the file into the tool that created it. Renaming a MOGRT or Adobe style to .cgpreset does not convert it.
  • Fonts and script: confirm the installed font includes the characters you need. The spoken-language selector chooses recognition; it does not translate or supply missing glyphs.
  • Real footage: check contrast, line breaks and motion on a bright shot, a busy shot and the longest phrase. The home page's prepared-sample demo helps explore settings but does not transcribe your footage or prove native performance.

Native style behavior: Adobe's caption styles and Track Styles and Linked Styles. Captions Gen preset names and settings were checked against the public 1.1.9 panels.

Questions people ask

Are there free caption templates for Premiere Pro?

Yes. Premiere includes Track Styles for native captions. A free MOGRT can supply a graphic design, though it doesn't transcribe speech by itself. Captions Gen is currently free with 18 built-in presets for animated caption graphics on Windows; the Adobe app is separate and paid.

What is the difference between a MOGRT and a caption preset?

A MOGRT is a Motion Graphics Template that can expose editable text and design controls; it doesn't generate a transcript by itself. In Captions Gen, a preset combines design, grouping and animation settings for captions generated from speech.

How do I save my own caption template in Premiere Pro?

In Premiere, style a native caption in Properties and save a Track Style. In Captions Gen, customize a look, open Save preset beside the preview, and choose Save as preset or Save as new preset. Its tile menu lets you export the preset to share.

Can I import a MOGRT or Adobe style as a Captions Gen preset?

No. A MOGRT, an Adobe Track Style or Linked Style, and a Captions Gen .cgpreset are different formats. A .cgpreset shares the panel's design and grouping settings, not a transcript, media, font files or an installed speech model.

Do the templates work in other languages?

Captions Gen offers searchable spoken-language selection and compatible custom speech models. Accuracy and word timing vary by language and model. Choose a font that contains the script's characters and review the result; a preset does not translate speech.

Is there a watermark or time limit?

The current free Captions Gen release has no watermark or transcription time limit. The direct installer download needs no email address or payment card. Paid features are planned for the future.
