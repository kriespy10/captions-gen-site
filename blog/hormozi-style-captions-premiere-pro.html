Hormozi style captions in Premiere Pro

Published 22 October 2026 · Updated 9 November 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Premiere Pro guide. Using After Effects? Install its panel and create your first captions.

To recreate Hormozi style captions in Premiere Pro, use heavy white caps, a dark outline and a yellow highlight that follows the spoken word. Keep the phrases short enough to read at phone size.

You can build the highlight by hand with graphics, or start from Captions Gen's currently free Bold Impact preset on Windows. The settings below are a starting point for the look, not a claim about every caption in Alex Hormozi's videos.

In short

  • Font: Montserrat Black or ExtraBold, all caps.
  • Style: white text, thick black outline, a soft shadow.
  • Highlight: the spoken word turns yellow (or green) and pops a little.
  • Length: two or three words on screen at a time.
  • Premiere's native Single Word layout shows one word at a time. For a moving highlight within a line, use timed graphics or Captions Gen.

What Hormozi style captions are

Editors use "Hormozi style" for short phrases in heavy caps with a contrasting spoken-word highlight. The look is associated with Alex Hormozi's social videos, but you can adapt the font, color and timing to your own channel.

Two or three words leave room for a large font. The moving color gives the reader a cue, but the caption still needs to make sense when paused. Read a whole sentence across the cuts: an isolated word can look tidy while hiding the meaning.

The font

The look needs a heavy, wide font in all caps. These free fonts all work:

  • Montserrat Black: wide, round and very bold.
  • Montserrat ExtraBold: a little lighter if Black feels too heavy.
  • Anton: tall and narrow. Fits more letters on a vertical frame.

Find Montserrat and Anton on Google Fonts. Install the needed weight, then restart Premiere Pro so it appears in the font list. A font name alone does not guarantee that another computer has the same font file.

Tip: Captions Gen bundles Montserrat and Anton. Bold Impact uses The Bold Font if you already have it installed, with Montserrat Black as a fallback. The website preview uses that fallback.

How the word highlight works

The highlight follows word timings, not just the beginning of each caption clip. Automatic timings are a draft to check against the voice, especially around quick speech, names and edits.

In the manual method, you can split a three-word graphic into three timed segments and change which word is yellow in each segment. The more speech you include, the more color changes you need to maintain.

Method 1: by hand in Premiere Pro

This works with Premiere alone. It's fine for one short video, but slow if you post often.

  1. Transcribe. Open Window > Text, then click Transcribe sequence.
  2. Create short captions. Click Create captions and set a low maximum length so you get two or three words per caption.
  3. Style the track. Select the captions, open Window > Properties, and set the font, caps, white fill, black stroke and shadow. Save it as a Track Style.
  4. Turn captions into graphics. Select the captions and choose Graphics and Titles > Upgrade Caption to Graphic. Keep a copy of the native caption track if you need SRT or closed captions.
  5. Color each word. In each graphic, make the spoken word yellow. Cut the clip where the next word starts and move the color along.
  6. Add the pop. Keyframe the scale from about 100% to 110% and back over a few frames.

Note: Steps 5 and 6 need manual timing. Changing the words or the cut may mean revisiting those segments and keyframes.

Make it your own

Open the Customize tab to change the look. The changes show in the preview right away.

  • Highlight color: switch yellow to green, red or blue.
  • Max words: how many words show at once. Two or three is the classic look.
  • Text size and Highlight size: how big the text is and how much the spoken word grows.
  • Position from top: move the captions up or down.
  • Animation: how each group comes in, such as Pop or Bounce.

Open Save preset beside the preview and choose Save as preset or Save as new preset. To make it the starting look, open its ⋯ menu and choose Make main preset.

A taller version

The Punch preset is the same idea in Anton, a tall and narrow font. It fits longer words on a vertical frame.

Where to place them

On vertical video, the app's buttons cover parts of the frame. Put your captions a little below the middle, away from the bottom and the right edge.

Fix words without losing the rhythm

In 1.1.9, a text correction and a style change are different jobs. Use this order rather than regenerating everything for every small change:

  1. Correct the words. Use the caption editor. If you edit a graphic's text through Premiere's Properties instead, the existing animation does not automatically know the new wording.
  2. Update animation. Use the panel's update action when offered, then listen to the affected phrase. Replacement words can receive estimated timing, so check the first and last highlighted word.
  3. Restyle separately. Choose a look and use Apply style for appearance changes. Changing grouping can require transcription again; it is not just a color change.

Need the same broad look in an After Effects composition? Use the separate After Effects word-by-word workflow. Its native layers have a different editing workflow from Premiere graphics.

Common mistakes

  • Too many words. The look falls apart as a full sentence. Keep it to three words or fewer.
  • Color without a rule. Keep one spoken-word color unless a deliberate change communicates something. Make sure the text is still legible without relying on color alone.
  • A thin outline. Over a bright shot the white text disappears. Keep the outline thick.
  • Typos in names. In Captions Gen Settings, turn on Check transcript before generating. Review names before placement, then play through the final graphics.

"Hormozi style" describes the look; Captions Gen isn't affiliated with Alex Hormozi. Native workflow references: Adobe's caption styles in Properties and Single Word captions.

Questions people ask

What font is used for Hormozi captions?

Montserrat Black or ExtraBold gives you a bold, wide look; Anton gives taller, narrower letters. These are free Google Fonts. Captions Gen's Bold Impact preset tries The Bold Font first if installed, then falls back to Montserrat Black.

Can Premiere Pro make Hormozi captions without a plugin?

Yes, with manual graphics work. Premiere can transcribe and create native captions, including a Single Word layout in current versions. To move a yellow highlight across a multi-word line, upgrade captions to graphics and time the color changes yourself.

Is there a free Hormozi captions preset for Premiere Pro?

Yes. Captions Gen is currently free for Premiere Pro 25.6 or newer on supported 64-bit Windows. Bold Impact gives heavy caps with a yellow spoken word; Punch gives a taller version. The installer downloads directly, with no email requirement, payment card, watermark or time limit.

Can I change the yellow to green?

Yes. In Captions Gen, open the Customize tab and pick a different highlight color. Save it as your own preset so every video uses it.

Can I still edit the captions after generating them?

Yes. Captions Gen places editable Motion Graphics clips on the Premiere timeline. In 1.1.9, correct the words in the caption editor and use Update animation when offered. Direct Properties text edits also need the animation update. New word timings can be estimated, so review the highlight against the voice.

Does it work on Mac or in CapCut?

This public Captions Gen release is for Windows, with Premiere Pro and After Effects panels. There is no public Mac installer or CapCut plugin. The manual Premiere method in this guide can also be used on a Mac.
