Hormozi-style captions in Premiere Pro

Updated 22 October 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Quick answer: "Hormozi-style" captions are heavy uppercase words, two or three at a time, white with a thick black outline, and the word being spoken flips to yellow or green with a small pop. Premiere Pro can't highlight the spoken word on a caption track, so you either build each word as a graphic by hand or use a plugin that generates them. Captions Gen does it in one click with its free Bold Impact preset.

What makes the look

  • Font: a heavy geometric sans in all caps. Montserrat Black or ExtraBold is the usual pick; Anton works for a taller, condensed version.
  • Few words: two or three words on screen, centred in the lower-middle of a vertical frame, so the eye never has to read a full line.
  • Contrast: white fill, a thick black stroke and a hard drop shadow, so the text reads over any shot.
  • The spoken word: changes color (yellow and green are the classics) and scales up slightly the moment it is said.
  • Snappy timing: each group of words appears exactly on the first syllable and leaves on the last.

Doing it by hand in Premiere Pro

  1. Transcribe the sequence and create captions with a short maximum length (Text panel, Captions, Create captions, then set the line length low).
  2. Style the caption track: Montserrat Black, all caps, white, a 6 to 10 px black stroke and a shadow. Save it as a Track Style.
  3. For the highlight, right-click the captions and choose Upgrade Caption to Graphic. Each caption becomes a graphic clip.
  4. In every graphic, color the spoken word and keyframe a small scale-up. Then split each clip at the start of the next word and move the color along.

Steps 1 and 2 take a few minutes. Step 4 is the slow part: a 60-second short has around 150 words, so it means a few hundred cuts and color changes, and a retake means doing them again.

Doing it with Captions Gen (free)

  1. Install Captions Gen (Windows 10/11, Premiere Pro 25.0 or newer) and open it from Window, UXP Plugins.
  2. Select the clip with the voice in your timeline.
  3. On the Presets tab, tap Bold Impact. The preview shows the look on sample words.
  4. Optional: on Customize, switch the highlight to green, change the size or how many words show at once, then save it as your own preset.
  5. Press Generate. Captions Gen transcribes on your computer and places word-timed, editable graphics on the track above your clip.

Want it taller and more condensed? The Punch preset uses Anton with a yellow spoken word. Prefer green? Karaoke turns the spoken word green over two outlined lines.

Tips that make it look right

  • Keep it to three words or fewer. The look falls apart as a full sentence.
  • Place the captions in the middle third of a 9:16 frame, above TikTok's and Reels' bottom buttons.
  • Use one highlight color per video. Switching between yellow and green in one edit looks accidental.
  • Check the transcript for names and jargon before you style anything. Fixing a word after the graphics exist takes longer.

"Hormozi-style" is the name editors use for this look, after the videos that made it popular. Captions Gen isn't affiliated with him; the preset is simply named after what it looks like.
