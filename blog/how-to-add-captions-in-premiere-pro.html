How to Add Captions in Premiere Pro

Updated 2026-08-26 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Premiere Pro has had captions built in since 2021, and for a plain subtitle track it's genuinely good. You don't need a plugin, a template pack, or a trip to another app. Here's the whole workflow, plus the styling limits you'll run into and what to do about them.

Step 1: Transcribe your sequence

Open Window > Text, switch to the Transcript tab, and click Transcribe sequence. Premiere's speech-to-text runs on your machine and takes roughly the length of the clip or less. Pick the spoken language (or leave auto-detect), and if you have separate dialogue tracks, point it at the right audio track — it saves cleanup later.

Step 2: Create captions from the transcript

In the same panel, hit Create captions. The dialog that matters:

  1. Format: pick Subtitle for normal on-screen captions. (More on closed captions below.)
  2. Max characters per line: the default 42 reads like TV subtitles. For a short-form look with 3–5 words per caption, drop it to 16–20.
  3. Lines: single or double. Short-form is almost always single.

Premiere adds a caption track above your video with every caption timed to the transcript. Fix any misheard words by double-clicking the caption text — the timing stays put.

Step 3: Style the track

Select a caption and open Window > Essential Graphics. Font, size, color, outline, background box — set them once, then save it as a Track Style so every caption on the track follows. This is also the honest limit of native captions: one style for the whole track. There's no per-word color, no pop-in, no karaoke highlight. Every caption looks identical.

Step 4: Export

In the export dialog, the Captions tab gives you two useful options: Burn captions into video (the text becomes pixels — what you want for Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts) or Create sidecar file (a separate .srt — what you want for YouTube's own caption system or a client hand-off).

What about closed captions?

If a broadcaster or platform asked you for closed captions (CEA-608/708), choose that format instead of Subtitle in step 2. Closed captions live in a data stream the viewer can toggle, and their styling is deliberately restricted — don't fight it. For everything social, subtitles burned in are the standard.
