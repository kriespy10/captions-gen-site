MrBeast style captions in Premiere Pro

Updated 22 October 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

MrBeast style captions are loud, fun and hard to miss. A comic font, one or two words at a time, a very thick outline, and the spoken word in a bright color that bounces in.

This guide covers the font, the settings, the bounce, and how to make the look in Premiere Pro: by hand, or free in one click.

In short

  • Font: a comic font like Bangers or Luckiest Guy, all caps.
  • Style: a very thick black outline and a hard shadow with no blur.
  • Highlight: the spoken word in neon green, yellow or cyan.
  • Motion: each word bounces in, growing past full size and settling.
  • Free option: the Beast Comic preset in Captions Gen.

What MrBeast style captions are

The look comes from the high-energy style of MrBeast's videos and the shorts cut from them. It feels like a comic book: big letters, bold colors and a lot of movement.

It's built to grab attention in the first seconds of a short, which is why gaming, challenge and reaction channels use it a lot.

The font

You need a bold, slightly playful font. These two free fonts are the usual picks:

  • Bangers: narrow comic letters with a slant. Closest to the classic look.
  • Luckiest Guy: round and chunky. Feels more like a game.

Both are on Google Fonts. Restart Premiere Pro after you install them.

The bounce

The bounce is what makes the captions feel alive. Each word starts small, grows a little past full size, then settles.

That small overshoot is the trick. Without it, the word just grows and looks stiff.

Method 1: by hand in Premiere Pro

  1. Install the font. Get Bangers or Luckiest Guy and restart Premiere.
  2. Transcribe and create captions. In Window > Text, transcribe the sequence and create captions with the shortest line length.
  3. Turn captions into graphics. Right-click them and choose Upgrade Caption to Graphic.
  4. Style one graphic. Comic font, all caps, thick black stroke, hard shadow. Save it as a Master Style and apply it to the rest.
  5. Add the bounce. For each word, keyframe Scale: 80% on the first frame, 110% on frame 3, 100% on frame 5.
  6. Color the spoken word. Make it green, and move the color along as the next word starts.

Note: This looks great but takes a long time. The bounce and color need setting for every word, and a 60-second video has around 150 of them.

Tips for better results

  • Give big words their own screen. Numbers, money and names hit harder alone.
  • Go bigger in the hook. Raise the text size for the first three seconds, then bring it back down.
  • Keep the outline thick. On a busy background a thick outline reads better than a box behind the text.
  • Mind the buttons. On Shorts, Reels and TikTok, keep captions above the bottom fifth of the frame.

Questions people ask

What font is used for MrBeast captions?

Editors usually recreate the look with a bold comic font such as Bangers or Luckiest Guy. Both are free on Google Fonts.

How do I make words bounce in Premiere Pro?

Keyframe the Scale of each word: about 80% on the first frame, about 110% two or three frames later, then 100%. Captions Gen does this for every word automatically.

Is there a free MrBeast captions preset for Premiere Pro?

Yes. Captions Gen is a free plugin for Premiere Pro on Windows. Its Beast Comic preset uses Bangers with a green bouncing word, and Gamer uses Luckiest Guy with a cyan word.

Should I use MrBeast style captions on long YouTube videos?

Use them for the big moments, like the hook, numbers and reveals. For a whole long video, a calmer style such as Podcast or Subtitle Box is easier to watch.

Does it work on Mac?

Captions Gen is Windows only for now. On a Mac you can still build the look by hand with the steps in this guide.
