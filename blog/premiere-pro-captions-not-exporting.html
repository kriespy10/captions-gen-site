Premiere Pro captions not exporting

Published 26 August 2026 · Updated 9 November 2026 · by Kris, maker of Captions Gen

Premiere Pro guide. Using After Effects? Install its panel and create your first captions.

If native captions are missing from the exported picture, open Export > Captions and check Export Options. Choose Burn Captions Into Video for visible words in the picture. A sidecar or embedded stream needs a compatible receiving workflow. For graphics on video tracks, check their video output instead of native caption export settings.

First identify what is missing

Compare the request with the files you actually exported. An MP4 plus an SRT can be a correct sidecar delivery even when opening only the MP4 shows no words. Conversely, text visible in a video does not prove that a required selectable caption stream exists.

Native text should be visible in the video

  1. Open the same sequence that contains the caption track. Park the playhead over a caption and confirm it is visible in the Program Monitor.
  2. Check the track's output and the caption clip's time range. Enable only the caption tracks intended for this picture version.
  3. In Export, expand Captions and choose Burn Captions Into Video. Review the listed caption tracks and export range.
  4. Export a short passage to a new, clearly named file. Open that new file, rather than an older export cached in a player.

If captions are missing in the preview too, first use the preview visibility guide. Export settings cannot reveal a caption outside its clip duration or repair text placed outside the frame.

A sidecar exported, but the player shows nothing

Find the actual caption file and confirm that its language and timing match this video. Load it explicitly in a compatible player or upload it using the destination's caption-file controls. A separate SRT does not become pixels in an MP4 just because the files share a folder.

If you want a video that always carries the visual treatment, make a separate burned-in version. Keep the clean video and sidecar when the delivery also requires selectable subtitles. The SRT guide covers import, encoding and offsets.

Embedded captions are absent or unavailable

The caption format and output container determine which embedded options appear. Check both against the recipient's specification. Then test the output in a player that supports the embedded caption stream and enable captions there.

Do not assume every MP4 export supports the requested broadcast caption format. Adobe documents specific container and caption combinations. For CEA-608, CEA-708 and required channel/service choices, follow the closed-caption checklist.

Animated graphics are missing

Captions Gen output, Type-tool titles and upgraded captions live on video tracks. Their visibility depends on the graphic clip, its layers and the video track. Keep them inside the export range and check for an opaque layer above them. The native Captions section does not control those graphics.

If you can see the design in the sequence but not in a short export, check the selected source sequence, range and graphic visibility before changing transcription settings. A new transcript cannot repair a disabled video track.

Check queued jobs and the export range

When using Media Encoder, inspect that queued job's settings and source. Do not assume a previously queued job adopts every later edit. Use a newly queued short sample after changing the sequence or caption options.

Test the opening, a middle section and the ending of the final delivery. Verify the intended language track, visible text and sidecar separately. A successful short test narrows the problem; it does not certify every later caption or another output preset.

Export Frame is a separate case

A still image exported from the Program Monitor is different from exporting the sequence video. Adobe lists a caption-in-Export-Frame issue for Premiere 25.6.1 and gives upgrading the affected caption to a graphic as a workaround. Do not treat that version-specific still-image note as the diagnosis for all missing video captions. Preserve native captions before any conversion.
